Two tests, two parts

Test Variable types Command
Difference in proportions Nominal outcome prtest
Difference in means Numeric outcome ttest

Test 1: Difference in proportions

  • Research question: Are countries with a British colonial history more likely to be democracies today than countries with a French colonial history?
  • Null Hypothesis (H0): There is no systematic difference between British- and French-colonised countries in their likelihood of being democracies.
  • Alternative Hypothesis (H1): Countries with a British colonial history are more likely to be democratic than countries with a French colonial history.

Test 1 example: Step 1 — descriptive

keep if ht_colonial == 5 | ht_colonial == 6

tabulate ht_colonial bmr_dem, row nofreq
  • Restricts to British (5) and French (6) colonised countries.
  • Row percentages show the share of democracies in each group.

Test 1 example: Step 2 — run the test

prtest bmr_dem, by(ht_colonial)
  • diff — estimated gap in proportions.
  • 95% CI excludes 0 → reject the null.

Test 1: Your turn

  • Research question: Are elections in dictatorships more likely to be followed by protests?
  • Variables: bmr_dem (regime type) and nelda_rpae (protests after elections).
  • State the null hypothesis (\(H_0\)) and the alternative hypothesis (\(H_1\)), then run the test using prtest.

Test 1: Your turn — setup

use "data-raw/Quality_of_Government_2026.dta", clear

drop if nelda_rpae == 99

label define bmr_dem_lbl 1 "Democracy" 0 "Dictatorship"
label values bmr_dem bmr_dem_lbl

tabulate bmr_dem nelda_rpae, row nofreq

Test 1: Your turn — questions

  • What share of dictatorships have post-election protests?
  • Run the test. Does the 95% CI include zero?
  • Do we reject or fail to reject the null?

Test 2: Difference in means

  • Research question: Is turnout higher in countries with proportional systems than in countries with majoritarian systems?
  • Null Hypothesis (H0): Mean voter turnout is the same in majoritarian and proportional electoral systems.
  • Alternative Hypothesis (H1): Proportional systems have higher turnout than majoritarian systems.

Test 2 example: Step 1 — descriptive

keep if gol_est == 1 | gol_est == 2

by gol_est, sort: summarize ideavt_legvt
  • Restricts to majoritarian (1) and proportional (2) systems.
  • Group means show average turnout in each system type.

Test 2 example: Step 2 — run the test

ttest ideavt_legvt, by(gol_est)
  • diff — estimated gap in mean turnout.
  • 95% CI includes 0 → fail to reject the null.

Test 2: Your turn

  • Research question: Is income inequality higher in dictatorships than in democracies?
  • Variables: bmr_dem (regime type) and wdi_gini (Gini index).
  • State the null hypothesis (\(H_0\)) and the alternative hypothesis (\(H_1\)), then run the test using ttest.

Test 2: Your turn — setup

use "data-raw/Quality_of_Government_2026.dta", clear

label define bmr_dem_lbl 1 "Democracy" 0 "Dictatorship"
label values bmr_dem bmr_dem_lbl

summarize wdi_gini

by bmr_dem, sort: summarize wdi_gini

Test 2: Your turn — questions

  • What is the mean Gini for dictatorships? For democracies?
  • Run the test. Does the 95% CI include zero?
  • Do we reject or fail to reject H0?

Why this matters

  • Descriptive analysis shows us patterns in our data.
  • Hypothesis testing tells us whether those patterns are larger than chance.
  • prtest and ttest are the building blocks for inference in PS2.
