From inference to regression

  • Last week: we tested whether two groups differ in a mean or proportion.
  • This week: we describe and test a linear relationship between two numeric variables.

Regression 1: Education and the Leave vote

  • Research question: Do regions with higher education shares vote Leave less?
  • H0: No linear relationship between education share and Leave vote share.
  • H1: Higher education share is associated with a lower Leave vote share.

Step 1: Produce the scatterplot

twoway (scatter leave_share share_high_skill) ///
       (lfit leave_share share_high_skill), leg(off)
  • Each dot is a UK region.
  • The line shows the estimated linear relationship.

Step 2: Run the regression

  • regress fits a bivariate regression: outcome on one predictor.
regress leave_share share_high_skill
  • leave_share is the dependent variable (y).
  • share_high_skill is the independent variable (x).

Reading the output: the coefficient table

Element What it tells you
Coefficient Estimated slope (β̂₁) or intercept (β̂₀)
P>|t| p-value
[95% conf. interval] Plausible range for the true coefficient

Reading the output: top-right block

Element What it tells you
Number of obs Sample size used in the regression
R-squared Share of variance in y explained by x

Writing the regression equation

  • _cons ≈ 83.92 — this is the intercept (β̂₀).
  • share_high_skill ≈ −1.13 — this is the slope (β̂₁).

\[\widehat{\text{Leave}} = 83.92 - 1.13 \times \text{HigherEd}\]

Interpreting the slope

  • For each 1 percentage point increase in higher education share…
  • …Leave vote share is expected to decrease by 1.13 percentage points.
  • The negative sign confirms the direction seen in the scatterplot.

Assessing statistical significance

  • p-value ≈ 0.000 → well below the 0.05 threshold.
  • 95% CI: [−1.42, −0.84] → does not include zero.
  • We reject H0: the relationship is statistically significant.

Reporting R-squared

  • R-squared ≈ 0.30.
  • About 30% of the variation in Leave vote share is explained by education share.
  • The remaining 70% is explained by other factors not in this model.

Regression 2: Unemployment and the Leave vote

  • Research question: Do regions with higher unemployment vote Leave more?
  • Same procedure — one command change.
regress leave_share unemployment

Reading the output: non-significant result

Element Value Interpretation
Slope +0.19 Small positive estimate
p-value 0.655 Much larger than 0.05
95% CI [−0.64, +1.02] Includes zero
R-squared 0.001 Near-zero explanatory power

The decision rule: same logic, different outcome

  • 95% CI includes zero → fail to reject H0.
  • p-value = 0.655 → far above 0.05; result consistent with chance variation.
  • R-squared ≈ 0 → unemployment explains almost none of the variation in Leave vote.

Recap

  • regress y x fits a bivariate regression and produces slope, intercept, p-value, CI, and R-squared.
  • If the 95% CI for the slope excludes zero, reject the null hypothesis.
  • R-squared measures how much variation in y is explained by x.

Why this matters:

  • Regression is the core tool of quantitative social science.
  • Reading output correctly is an essential skill.
