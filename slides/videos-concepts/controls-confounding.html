Controls and Confounding

You’ll learn to

  • Explain why multivariate regression helps address confounding.
  • Interpret what happens when a coefficient “goes away.”
  • Recognise the limits of controlling for confounders.

Recall: Confounding variables

  • A confounding variable affects both X and Y, creating a spurious association.
  • We identified confounding as a threat to causal claims.
  • Multivariate regression lets us control for potential confounders.

How controlling works

  • Include the suspected confounder as a variable in the regression.
  • The coefficient on X now compares units with similar values of the confounder.
  • If X’s coefficient shrinks or disappears, confounding was likely present.

Example 1: Immigration and the Leave vote

  • Bivariate regression: regions with more immigrants voted Leave less.
  • Slope: \(b = -0.41\), statistically significant (\(p < 0.001\)).
  • But could education be driving both immigration and Leave voting?

Education as a confounder

  • Educated regions attract more immigrants (better economic opportunities).
  • Educated regions also voted Remain.
  • Education affects both the explanatory variable and the outcome.

Controlling for education

Bivariate Multivariate
Immigration -0.41 0.08
Education — -1.14
  • Immigration’s effect disappears once education is held constant.

Example 2: Health spending and CO2 emissions

  • Across countries, health expenditure strongly predicts CO2 emissions.
  • Slope: \(b = 0.73\) (\(p < 0.001\)). Countries spending more on health pollute more.
  • Does healthcare cause pollution? Obviously not. What is going on?

Controlling for GDP per capita

Bivariate Multivariate
Health spending 0.73 -0.04
GDP per capita — 0.91
  • The health-CO2 relationship vanishes.
  • National income drove both variables.

Does controlling for confounders prove causation?

  • No. “Confounding variables are everywhere.”
  • We can only control for variables we observe and think of.
  • Controls also do not address concerns about reverse causality.

Recap

  • Adding a confounder to a regression can reveal spurious relationships.
  • A coefficient that “goes away” suggests confounding was present.
  • Controls reduce confounding concerns but cannot establish causation.

Why this matters:

  • Most social science research uses observational data, not experiments.
  • Multivariate regression is the primary tool for addressing confounders.
  • Understanding its limits is essential for interpreting research critically.
