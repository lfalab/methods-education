Regression Slope

You’ll learn to

  • Identify the intercept and slope in a regression equation.
  • Explain where the slope comes from.
  • Interpret the slope and intercept in substantive terms.

Where we left off

\[\widehat{\text{Leave}} = 84 - 1.13 \times \text{HigherEd}\]

  • A regression equation is a linear function fitted to data.
  • But where do 84 and −1.13 come from?

The components of a regression equation

\[\hat{y} = \hat{\beta}_0 + \hat{\beta}_1 x\]

  • \(\hat{y}\) — dependent variable (predicted value)
  • \(x\) — independent variable

The components of a regression equation

\[\hat{y} = \hat{\beta}_0 + \hat{\beta}_1 x\]

  • \(\hat{\beta}_0\) — the intercept (or constant)
  • \(\hat{\beta}_1\) — the slope (or coefficient)

Where does the slope come from?

\[\hat{\beta}_1 = \frac{\text{Cov}(X, Y)}{\text{Var}(X)}\]

  • Numerator: covariance of X and Y — do they move together?
  • Denominator: variance of X — how spread out is X?

Intuition behind the formula

  • If X and Y tend to move in the same direction: positive slope.
  • If X and Y tend to move in opposite directions: negative slope.
  • We scale by Var(X) so the slope is expressed in the right units.

Interpreting the slope

  • For each 1-unit increase in x, y is expected to change by \(\hat{\beta}_1\).
  • Direction: the sign of the slope tells you positive or negative association.
  • Magnitude: the size tells you how much y changes per unit of x.

Slope: Brexit example

\[\hat{\beta}_1 = -1.13\]

  • For each 1 percentage point increase in higher education share…
  • …Leave vote share is expected to decrease by 1.13 percentage points.

Where does the intercept come from?

\[\hat{\beta}_0 = \bar{y} - \hat{\beta}_1 \bar{x}\]

  • \(\bar{y}\) — mean of the dependent variable
  • \(\bar{x}\) — mean of the independent variable

Interpreting the intercept

  • The intercept is the predicted value of y when x = 0.
  • Often this is not a meaningful real-world scenario.
  • It anchors the line — do not over-interpret it.

Intercept: Brexit example

\[\hat{\beta}_0 = 84\]

  • Predicted Leave share when higher education share = 0%.
  • No UK region has 0% higher education — this is outside the data.
  • The intercept is a mathematical anchor, not a substantive prediction.

Residuals: the line is never perfect

Residuals: the line is never perfect

  • Each region’s actual Leave vote differs from its predicted value.
  • The vertical distance from a dot to the line is the residual (or error).
  • Larger residuals mean the regression predicts that region less well.

Ordinary Least Squares

  • The slope and intercept are chosen to minimise the sum of squared residuals.
  • This quantity is called the Residual Sum of Squares (RSS).
  • Because we minimise squared residuals: Ordinary Least Squares (OLS).

Recap

  • The slope = Cov(X, Y) / Var(X) — how y changes per unit of x.
  • The intercept is the predicted y when x = 0.
  • OLS finds the line that minimises the sum of squared residuals.

Why this matters:

  • Every regression result you read in a paper rests on these calculations.
  • Knowing what the slope captures helps you evaluate whether it makes sense.
  • OLS is the standard estimation method across the social sciences.
