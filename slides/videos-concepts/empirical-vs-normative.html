Normative and Empirical Questions

Watch on YouTube

You’ll learn to

  • Distinguish normative and empirical questions.
  • Explain why we’ll focus on empirical questions.

Why care about this distinction?

  • Some questions ask what should be happening in the world.
  • Others ask what is happening.
  • Quantitative social science is concerned with the second type.

Normative questions

  • Normative questions involve value judgements.

    • Should income inequality be reduced?
    • Is compulsory voting desirable?
    • What is a fair tax system?

These questions matter, but they cannot be answered by data alone.

Empirical questions

  • Empirical questions ask about observable patterns and relationships.

    • How unequal is income across regions?
    • Does compulsory voting increase turnout?
    • How do tax changes affect employment?

These are the questions statistics helps us answer.

Good empirical questions

  • Besides avoiding normative inquiries, a good empirical question must meet additional criteria.

    • Avoid factual questions with a simple answer.
    • Avoid questions that we can’t answer with data (e.g., hypothetical questions about imagined scenarios.)

Avoiding factual questions

  • Some questions may be important, but too easy to answer.

    • Which UK regions voted most heavily for Brexit?
    • Which party governed the US from 1981 to 1992?

If you can find the answer with a quick Google search, think of a different question.

Focusing on observables

  • Other questions are impossible to answer.

    • If Bernie Sanders had beaten Hillary Clinton in the 2016 Democratic primary, would he have beaten Trump in the general election?
    • Did ancient Chinese explorers visit the Americas without leaving any trace of their presence?

We must save our efforts for the realm of possibility.

Recap

  • Normative questions ask what should be happening in the world.
  • Empirical questions ask what is happening.
  • Our exclusion of normative questions is methodological, not evaluative.

Why this matters:

  • A research method is never an end in itself.
  • Choosing an appropriate method for a research question is a core analytical skill.
