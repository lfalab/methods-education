QUESTION: A confounding variable is best defined as a variable that:

A. Lies on the causal pathway between X and Y
B. Affects both the independent and dependent variables and is not caused by the independent variable
C. Is caused by the independent variable
D. Affects only the dependent variable

ANSWER: B

---

QUESTION: A study finds that countries with higher ice cream sales have higher rates of violent crime. A researcher suggests that warm weather may be a confounding variable. Why would warm weather be considered a confounder?

A. Warm weather causes ice cream sales but not crime
B. Warm weather affects both ice cream sales and crime rates independently
C. Warm weather is caused by ice cream consumption
D. Warm weather lies on the pathway from ice cream sales to crime

ANSWER: B

---

QUESTION: A researcher observes that students who take notes on laptops perform worse on exams than students who handwrite notes. Prior academic ability could be a confounding variable if:

A. Prior ability affects both the choice of note-taking method and exam performance
B. Prior ability is caused by laptop use
C. Prior ability only affects exam performance
D. Prior ability lies on the pathway from note-taking to exam performance

ANSWER: A

---

QUESTION: Which of the following best describes the difference between a confounder and a mediator?

A. A confounder is caused by the independent variable; a mediator is not
B. A confounder influences both X and Y from outside the relationship; a mediator lies on the causal pathway from X to Y
C. A confounder only affects Y; a mediator only affects X
D. A confounder is always measured; a mediator is always unmeasured

ANSWER: B

---

QUESTION: A study finds that people who drink coffee have lower rates of depression. If coffee consumption reduces depression by improving sleep quality, what role does sleep quality play?

A. Sleep quality is a confounder
B. Sleep quality is a mediator
C. Sleep quality is an independent variable
D. Sleep quality is omitted variable bias

ANSWER: B

---

QUESTION: What is omitted variable bias?

A. When a mediating variable is included in the analysis
B. When a confounding variable is left out of the analysis, leading to misleading estimates
C. When the dependent variable is omitted from a regression
D. When too many variables are included in a model

ANSWER: B

---

QUESTION: A study finds that cities with more coffee shops have higher crime rates. If population density is a confounding variable, this means:

A. Population density causes coffee shops but not crime
B. Population density affects both the number of coffee shops and crime rates
C. Coffee shops cause population density
D. Population density is caused by crime rates

ANSWER: B

---

QUESTION: Reverse causality occurs when:

A. X and Y are uncorrelated
B. We assume X causes Y, but Y may actually cause X
C. A confounding variable affects both X and Y
D. The independent variable is measured incorrectly

ANSWER: B

---

QUESTION: A study finds that cities with more police officers have higher crime rates. Which statement best describes the reverse causality concern?

A. Police officers cause crime
B. Crime rates may prompt cities to hire more police, rather than police causing crime
C. A confounding variable affects both policing and crime
D. The correlation is spurious and meaningless

ANSWER: B

---

QUESTION: Researchers observe that countries with higher levels of education have stronger democracies. Why might reverse causality be a concern?

A. Education levels cannot affect democracy
B. Democracies may invest more in education, rather than education causing democracy
C. Education and democracy are unrelated
D. A third variable causes both education and democracy

ANSWER: B

QUESTION: A correlation shows that people who exercise more report better mental health. Which scenario represents reverse causality?

A. A confounding variable affects both exercise and mental health
B. People with better mental health may be more motivated to exercise
C. Exercise improves mental health through biological mechanisms
D. Exercise and mental health are unrelated

ANSWER: B

QUESTION: Why can correlations alone not determine causal direction?

A. Correlations are always spurious
B. Y may cause X, X may cause Y, or both directions may operate
C. Correlations only measure strength, not direction
D. Causal direction can only be determined by p-values

QUESTION: A study finds that students who attend office hours get higher grades. Both confounding and reverse causality could threaten this causal claim. Which describes the reverse causality concern?

A. Prior academic ability affects both office hour attendance and grades
B. Students who are already doing well may be more likely to attend office hours
C. Office hours directly cause grade improvement
D. A third variable causes both attendance and grades

ANSWER: B

QUESTION: What is the counterfactual problem in causal inference?

A. We can never collect enough data
B. We cannot observe the same unit in both treated and untreated states simultaneously
C. We cannot measure all variables perfectly
D. Correlations are always confounded

ANSWER: B

QUESTION: How does random assignment address confounding variables?

A. It eliminates all confounders from the study
B. It ensures treatment and control groups are similar on average, including on potential confounders
C. It measures confounders more accurately
D. It proves that confounders do not exist

ANSWER: B

---

QUESTION: In an experiment testing whether exposure to negative news increases anxiety, participants are randomly assigned to read either negative or neutral news stories. How does random assignment address reverse causality?

A. It proves that anxiety cannot cause news exposure
B. It ensures that pre-existing anxiety levels cannot cause exposure, because exposure is imposed by the researcher
C. It eliminates anxiety from the study
D. It measures the direction of causation directly

ANSWER: B

---

QUESTION: A researcher randomly assigns voters to receive either a campaign flyer or no flyer, then measures voting behaviour. What does random assignment achieve?

A. It guarantees that every participant votes
B. It ensures the treatment and control groups are comparable on average before the flyer is distributed
C. It eliminates all differences between participants
D. It proves that campaign flyers always increase turnout

ANSWER: B

QUESTION: Why can researchers not use random assignment to study whether poverty causes poor health outcomes?

A. Poverty and health are unrelated
B. It would be unethical and impractical to randomly assign people to poverty
C. Random assignment only works for small sample sizes

QUESTION: An experiment finds that students randomly assigned to receive tutoring score higher on exams than those who do not receive tutoring. What can we conclude?

A. Tutoring causes exam score improvement
