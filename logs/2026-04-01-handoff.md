## Key design decisions

### Why these specific stimuli

- **NSS histogram:** Left-skewed, which enables a mean vs median question. Students have seen it in the concept video (C09) but not in either real exam.
- **Leisure centre scenario:** Clear non-probability sampling with unambiguous bias direction (overestimates support for cycle lane because leisure centre users are more physically active).
- **Brexit regression table:** From the concept video (C28). Students have studied it before. Features a variable that loses significance in the multivariate model (Immigrant Share), enabling a significance comparison question.

### What was deliberately excluded

- **Reverse causality question** — removed because the Brexit case makes reverse causality awkward (education → Leave voting is hard to reverse plausibly, which could confuse students rather than test their understanding).
- **Topics left out of the mock exam:** quantitative vs qualitative reasoning (too open-ended), survey weights (too fine-grained for the exam), visual inference / CI overlap heuristic (hard to test without fabricating a plot), choosing bivariate tools (mechanical, tested in PS2).

### Point allocation rationale

Part 1 is 25 points (vs 15 in the actual exams) because it includes two extra question types (mean/median, descriptive/causal). Part 3 is 50 points (vs 60 in May) because the reverse causality question was removed.
