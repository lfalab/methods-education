Seminar Activity: Country-level Correlations

Statistics for Political Science — Week 5

Author

Leonardo Falabella

Introduction

This seminar has two parts. In the first hour, you will work through a set of variable pairs conceptually — identifying variable types, deciding which analytical case applies, and choosing the appropriate tool — without touching Stata. In the second hour, you will implement your choices in Stata and interpret the output.

Work through each part in order. Discuss your answers with your group before moving on.

The dataset for all exercises is the Quality of Government (QoG) dataset (available in our Shared Project Folder). Refer to the QoG codebook if you need more detail about any variable.

The Seven Variable Pairs

Below are seven pairs of variables drawn from the QoG dataset. Each pair is accompanied by a brief description of how each variable is measured and a research question that motivates the analysis. Read through all seven pairs before beginning Hour 1.

Pair 1

Variables: gol_est and ideavt_legvt

  • gol_est — Electoral system type (Bormann and Golder). Values: 1 = Majoritarian, 2 = Proportional, 3 = Mixed.
  • ideavt_legvt — Parliamentary election voter turnout (Institute for Democracy and Electoral Assistance). The percentage of the voting-age population that cast a vote in the most recent parliamentary election (0–100).

Research question: Do countries with different electoral systems differ in their average levels of voter turnout?

Pair 2

Variables: wdi_gini and ti_cpi

  • wdi_gini — Gini index (World Bank). A variable measuring income inequality within a country, ranging from 0 (perfect equality) to 100 (perfect inequality).
  • ti_cpi — Corruption Perceptions Index (Transparency International). A continuous variable ranging from 0 (highly corrupt) to 100 (highly clean), measuring perceptions of public sector corruption as seen by business people, risk analysts, and the general public.

Research question: Do more unequal countries tend to have higher levels of perceived corruption?

Pair 3

Variables: bmr_dem and ht_colonial

  • bmr_dem — Dichotomous democracy measure (Boix, Miller, and Rosato). This is a binary variable: 0 = not a democracy, 1 = democracy. Countries are coded as democratic if political leaders are chosen through free and fair elections and a minimum suffrage threshold is met. It does not measure degree of democracy.
  • ht_colonial — Colonial origin. A categorical variable recording the country’s former Western overseas colonial ruler (e.g. British, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Belgian). Countries that were never colonised by a Western overseas power are in a separate category.

Research question: Do countries with different colonial histories differ in their likelihood of being democratic today?

Pair 4

Variables: wdi_internet and rsf_pfi

  • wdi_internet — Individuals using the internet (World Bank). The percentage of the population with internet access (0–100).
  • rsf_pfi — Press Freedom Index (Reporters Without Borders). A numeric variable measuring the degree of freedom available to journalists and media organisations in each country. Higher scores indicate more press freedom.

Research question: Do countries with higher internet usage tend to have greater press freedom?

Pair 5

Variables: ht_colonial and ti_cpi

  • ht_colonial — Colonial origin. See Pair 3 for the full description.
  • ti_cpi — Corruption Perceptions Index. See Pair 2 for the full description.

Research question: Do countries with different colonial histories differ in their average levels of perceived corruption today?

Pair 6

Variables: gol_est and ht_region

  • gol_est — Electoral system type. See Pair 1 for the full description.
  • ht_region — Region of the world. A variable classifying countries into ten politico-geographic regions, including Eastern Europe and post-Soviet Union, Latin America, North Africa and the Middle East, Sub-Saharan Africa, Western Europe and North America, East Asia, South-East Asia, South Asia, the Pacific, and the Caribbean.

Research question: Do countries in different world regions tend to use different electoral systems?

Pair 7

Variables: wdi_gini and wdi_wip

  • wdi_gini — Gini index. See Pair 2 for the full description.
  • wdi_wip — Proportion of seats held by women in national parliaments (World Bank). Measured as a percentage (0–100).

Research question: Do more unequal countries tend to have lower levels of women’s representation in parliament?

Hour 1: Conceptual Mapping

In this hour, you will work through the seven pairs conceptually. Do not open Stata. For each pair, answer the three questions below by discussing with your group and writing down your answers.

For each of the seven pairs, answer:

Question A: What type is each variable?

Decide whether each variable is nominal (unordered categories), ordinal (ordered categories), or numeric (interval or ratio). Pay close attention to how the variable is measured — a variable coded as numbers is not necessarily numeric. Refer to the descriptions above and the codebook if needed.

Question B: Which analytical case applies?

Based on the variable types, determine which case this pair belongs to:

  • Case 1: Both variables are nominal → cross-tabulation and bivariate bar chart
  • Case 2: One nominal, one numeric → compare means and bar chart of means
  • Case 3: Both numeric → scatterplot and regression line

Question C: What is the appropriate tool?

State the specific plot or table you would use.

Once your group has worked through all seven pairs, be ready to discuss your answers. Pay particular attention to any pairs where you were uncertain — these are worth discussing carefully before moving to Hour 2.

Hour 2: Stata Implementation

In this hour, you will implement the tools you identified in Hour 1 and interpret the output. Work through the pairs in order, using bivariate-lecture.do as a reference for command syntax. For each pair, there are two steps: execute and interpret.

Pair 1 — gol_est and ideavt_legvt

Execute: Produce the appropriate summary table and chart.

Interpret:

  • Which electoral system is associated with the highest average turnout? Which with the lowest?
  • Is the difference between systems substantively meaningful, or are the means fairly similar?
  • Why might the choice of electoral system be associated with turnout? Think about how different systems create incentives for parties to mobilise voters.

Hint: A difference of a few percentage points in turnout can be politically significant at the national level, even if it looks modest in a chart.

Pair 2 — wdi_gini and ti_cpi

Execute: Produce the appropriate chart, including a regression line.

Interpret:

  • What is the direction of the association? Does a higher Gini index tend to be associated with higher or lower corruption scores?
  • Before interpreting the slope, make sure you have the scale direction of ti_cpi clearly in mind: a higher score means less corruption, not more. What does this imply about the slope you expect to see?

Hint: Getting the scale direction of ti_cpi right is essential for interpreting the slope correctly. Confirm which end of the scale represents more corruption before drawing conclusions.

Pair 3 — bmr_dem and ht_colonial

Execute: Produce the appropriate table and chart. When producing the table, try running it with both row percentages and column percentages.

Interpret:

  • Compare the row percentage output and the column percentage output. What does each tell you? Which is more useful for answering the research question, and why?
  • Are some colonial origins more strongly associated with democratic status than others? Which patterns stand out?

Hint: Row percentages show the distribution of one variable within each category of the other. Switching between row and column orientations reveals different aspects of the same association — think carefully about which question each answers.

Pair 4 — wdi_internet and rsf_pfi

Execute: Produce the appropriate chart, including a regression line.

Interpret:

  • Is there a positive association, a negative association, or no clear pattern?
  • Remember that higher rsf_pfi scores indicate more press freedom. If the slope is positive, what does that mean substantively?

Hint: Make sure you have the direction of the rsf_pfi scale clear before interpreting the slope. A positive slope here has a specific substantive meaning that depends on understanding what higher values represent.

Pair 5 — ht_colonial and ti_cpi

Execute: Produce the appropriate summary table and chart.

Interpret:

  • Which colonial-origin groups have the highest and lowest average corruption scores? Are the differences large or small?
  • Are there any groups represented by very few countries? How might this affect the reliability of the group mean?
  • Compare your findings here with Pair 2, which also involves ti_cpi. Both pairs ask about correlates of corruption, but use a different variable as the grouping factor. What does the comparison suggest about the sources of variation in corruption perceptions?

Hint: The bar chart of means lets you compare average corruption scores across colonial-origin categories at a glance. Look for categories with notably higher or lower means, and keep in mind that fewer countries in a category makes the average less reliable.

Pair 6 — gol_est and ht_region

Execute: Produce the appropriate table and chart.

Interpret:

  • Does electoral system type appear to be evenly distributed across regions, or does it cluster? Are there regions where one system type clearly dominates?
  • Are there any regions where data are sparse or missing? What might this imply?
  • Why might the choice of electoral system vary across world regions? Think about historical legacies, colonial influences, and the regional diffusion of institutional models.

Hint: A bivariate chart will make regional patterns easier to see than a table alone. Look for categories that are heavily concentrated in one region or largely absent from another.

Pair 7 — wdi_gini and wdi_wip

Execute: Produce the appropriate chart, including a regression line.

Interpret:

  • What does the slope of the regression line tell you about the direction of the relationship? Is a higher Gini index associated with more or fewer women in parliament?
  • Is the relationship strong or weak? How are the points distributed around the regression line?
  • Compare this with Pair 2, which also uses wdi_gini. Both pair inequality with a political outcome. Are the patterns similar or different? What might this suggest?

Hint: Pay attention to both the direction of the regression line and how tightly the points cluster around it. A flat or nearly flat line suggests little association, even if the slope is not exactly zero.

Summary

In this activity, you have:

  • Identified variable types and matched them to the appropriate bivariate analytical tool.
  • Produced and interpreted cross-tabulations, bar charts of means, and scatterplots with regression lines.
  • Connected descriptive patterns in data to theoretical arguments in political science.

These skills form the foundation for the independent empirical analysis you will carry out in Problem Set 2.
